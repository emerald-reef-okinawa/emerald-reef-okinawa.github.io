Should You Bring Your Phone Snorkeling in Okinawa? Water-Damage Risks & How to Get Great Photos Hands-Free

Published：2026.08.12
日本語繁體中文한국어

"I want to shoot with my phone while snorkeling" — "but I'm scared of water damage." The short answer: we don't recommend taking your phone into the sea. A local guide explains why, what to watch for if you use a waterproof case, and how to get pro-quality photos and video hands-free.

The verdict: we don't recommend taking your phone into the sea

Straight to the point: we don't recommend taking your own phone into the sea while snorkeling. The reason is simple — "waterproof" on a phone does not mean "usable in the ocean." Most recent iPhones and Androids are IP68 rated, but that test means fresh water, 1–2 m deep, for about 30 minutes. Salt water corrodes the inside far more easily, waves add impact, and most manufacturers exclude seawater use from the warranty. Every year, plenty of people enter the sea thinking "it's a waterproof phone" and end up with a dead one. Losing an expensive phone — and all the precious photos inside it — on your Okinawa trip is just too painful. That's a local guide's honest take.

Three risks of bringing a phone into the sea: damage, loss — and a smaller experience

There are three concrete risks. ① Water damage — a case left unsealed or with worn gaskets leaks more often than you'd think, and salt water does far more harm than fresh: even after drying, corrosion continues inside. ② Loss — drop it and, while sand is one thing, a phone that slips into a coral crevice is essentially unrecoverable. Leaving it on the beach means long stretches with your bag unattended. ③ The one people overlook: the experience itself shrinks. With one hand occupied you swim awkwardly, you view the sea through a screen, and you spend less time really taking in the fish and coral right in front of you. If you've come all the way to Okinawa's sea, keeping both hands free makes for a far richer memory.

If you still want to bring it: choosing a waterproof case & 3 golden rules

If you still want to shoot with your own phone, choose a dedicated IPX8 waterproof case (dry pouch) and stick to three rules. ① Before using it, seal a tissue inside and sink it in water to test for leaks. ② Pick one with a neck strap that also floats, and always secure it to your neck or wrist. ③ Don't use ultra-cheap cases or last year's old one — degraded gaskets are the number-one cause of leaks. Also, many phones won't register touch underwater, so set the volume button as the shutter beforehand. Honestly though, shooting through a case often fogs up or catches droplets, so image quality suffers and you rarely get the shot you pictured. Leaving the camera work to a pro and focusing on enjoying the moment tends to be the most satisfying way to spend the day.
