The Next Step After Snorkeling: Three Things Only Skin Diving Can Give You

Published：2026.09.10
日本語繁體中文한국어

The sea you look down on from the surface and the sea you drop into under your own power are two different places. Skin diving means no tank — just your breath. No licence is needed once you are taught how to breathe, and anyone from age 10 can try. For anyone who has enjoyed snorkeling even once, here is what only free-diving on a single breath can give you.

From looking down on it to being inside it

Snorkeling means floating on the surface and looking down at the sea. That alone is beautiful — but skin diving takes you inside that view. Drop to 3–5 metres and the coral stops being something below you and becomes something passing beside you. Shadowed rock, gaps in the coral, the rise and fall of the bottom — a sea that looked flat from above suddenly stands up as real three-dimensional terrain. Even at the same spot, people who have done both say the same thing: snorkeling and skin diving show you completely different oceans.

The sound stops, and the fish stop fleeing

The first thing that strikes you underwater is the silence. When you snorkel, the sound of your own breathing is right there in your ears the whole time; hold your breath and dive, and that sound vanishes — the sea goes startlingly quiet. The second thing: no bubbles means no alarmed fish. Without the big exhaust of scuba gear the schools do not scatter, and they will sometimes stream past close enough to touch. For as long as your breath lasts, you get to be one of the animals down there. That silence, and that sense of belonging, exist only on a single breath.

Age and fitness matter less than you think

"I am not fit enough." "Is that not a young person's thing?" We hear this often, and it simply is not true. Our age range is 10 to 59, and guests across that whole span join us. One of them wrote: "I am not young, but I really wanted to try skin diving, so I signed up. They coached me patiently and seriously until my jackknife dive started to take shape. The light as you rise back to the surface is irresistible." What matters is not strength but relaxation — the softer you go, the better you float and the longer your breath lasts. No licence required: we teach breathing, equalising, and the jackknife (tipping head-down from the surface) from scratch.

How the session runs, and what it costs — solo bookings welcome

Plan on about 60 minutes in the water. We practise breathing on land and in the shallows first, check that you can equalise, then work gradually deeper. Mask and long fins are free rentals, with free pickup from hotels in the Onna area. Pricing is ¥16,000 for one person, ¥10,000 each for two, ¥9,000 each for three or more — and we happily take solo bookings. We shoot with two cameras, the guide's and yours, and the photo data is free (video free for a limited time). You never get to see yourself diving, so the photos that arrive afterwards tend to surprise people. If conditions turn, safety comes first and we move to a spot that suits your experience level. Just get in touch and ask.
