Respect for the Aged Day: Take Three Generations into the Okinawa Sea — How to Choose a Snorkel Tour Grandparents Can Enjoy Too

Published：2026.09.09
日本語繁體中文한국어

September 21, 2026 is Respect for the Aged Day — and this year it falls inside a rare five-day Silver Week, making it ideal timing for a three-generation trip to Okinawa. Before you write the idea off with "the grandparents cannot swim," here is how a family with mixed ages and abilities can all enjoy the sea together.

2026: Respect for the Aged Day lands inside Silver Week

Respect for the Aged Day 2026 falls on Monday, September 21 — right in the middle of the five-day holiday running September 19 to 23. A five-day September break has not happened since 2015, eleven years ago, which makes it about the best possible timing for family who live apart to gather. We have seen a steady rise in three-generation bookings: grandparents, parents and children together. A gift is lovely, but a shared experience tends to outlast anything wrapped in paper. Time spent holding a grandchild's hand in the water is a present that photos can only hint at.

Why non-swimmers are fine: life jackets and shallow water

So many people give up on the idea because "the grandparents cannot swim" — but snorkeling requires no swimming ability at all. A life jacket floats you naturally at the surface. No kicking, no arm strokes: you simply relax, float, and put your face in the water, and the underwater world opens up. We provide a free life jacket for every participant and let you feel what floating is like in shallow water before heading out. We have guided guests from age 3 to 65. You can start at knee depth and take it slowly, and if anyone wants to get out, we go back — we never push.

A private tour means each person keeps their own pace

The biggest obstacle in a three-generation trip is the difference in pace. The grandchild wants to swim out; the grandparents want to watch fish slowly in the shallows. On a shared tour, someone always compromises. Every Emerald Reef tour is fully private — we guide only the family that booked, so we can match each person's pace within the same group. Grandparents can stay in the shallows with a float ring while a grandchild heads a little further out with the guide. One three-generation family told us: "My mother enjoyed it safely holding onto a float, and my daughter was absorbed in learning the names of the fish." Up to six people per group, with fish feeding included on every plan.

Pricing — and the family photo you get to keep

On pricing, everyone aged 3–12 pays the same flat rate: for the snorkeling plan that is ¥4,000 per child, and ¥6,000 per adult for groups of three or more — very manageable across three generations. And here is something three-generation trips always run into: there is never a photo with everyone in it, because somebody is always behind the camera. We shoot with two cameras — the guide's and yours — and send you all the photo data free (video data is free for a limited time too). A shot of grandparents and grandchildren floating side by side is the kind you will be glad to have. Hotel pickup is free, so nobody has to worry about driving a rental car either. Ask us anything before you book.
