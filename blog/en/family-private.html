Five Reasons Families Should Choose a Fully Private Tour — Even If a Child Cries or Nobody Swims the Same

Published：2026.09.25
日本語繁體中文한국어

A day at the sea with kids rarely goes to plan. Someone starts crying, someone needs the toilet, the older one wants to swim further out while the younger one is scared. On a shared tour, every one of those moments comes with a glance at the other guests. A fully private tour removes that pressure entirely — here is how, with words from families who joined us.

Reason 1: when a child cries, there is nobody to apologise to

A child who has never been in the sea starts crying right before getting in — it happens constantly. On a shared tour that comes with the pressure of holding everyone else up, and the parents end up more exhausted than the kids. On a private tour there is nobody to hold up. If they cry, take a break, play on the sand, and try again. One family wrote: "Our younger son got scared of the open water and cried, but the staff gently encouraged him and he made it through to the end." Not being rushed is exactly what gives a child room to try.

Reason 2: the gap in swimming ability inside one family stops being a problem

"Dad swims, Mum is nervous in water, the older one wants to head out, the younger one will not leave the shallows." That is simply what families look like. A shared tour moves everyone along the same route at the same pace, so somebody always compromises. A private tour lets you split the pace within one group — some watching fish in the shallows, others heading a little further out with the guide, at the same time. We take children from age 3, and floating in a life jacket is plenty of fun on its own.

Reasons 3 and 4: the guide watches only your family, and you get a photo with everyone in it

Safety is the other advantage. On a shared tour one guide watches more people; on a private tour that attention is all on your family. Having the guide right beside a child who is unsure in the water is worth more to a parent than anything else (up to six people per group). Then there are the photos. On family trips whoever holds the camera is always missing from the picture, and you come home without a single shot of everyone. We shoot with two cameras — the guide's and yours — and give you all the photo data free (video free for a limited time too), so Mum and Dad are in the underwater family photo as well.

Reason 5: for a family it is cheaper than it sounds — pickup included, nothing to bring

People assume private means expensive, but the per-person price falls as the group grows. Snorkeling in Onna is ¥6,000 per adult for three or more, and every child aged 3–12 pays a flat ¥4,000. Two adults and two children comes to ¥19,000 in total, including pickup, mask, fins, life jacket, fish feeding and photo data. With free hotel pickup there is no car seat to sort out and no rental car to keep clean. Bring a swimsuit and a towel — we take care of the part that makes travelling with kids hard.
