Why Beach Entry Beats a Boat: No Seasickness, and You Get In at Your Own Pace

Published：2026.09.25
日本語繁體中文한국어

Snorkeling in Okinawa comes in two forms: boat entry, where you head offshore by boat, and beach entry, where you simply walk in from the sand. Every Emerald Reef tour is beach entry. No seasickness, and you get used to the water gradually from the shallows — here is why that suits small children, older guests and nervous swimmers best.

The biggest difference: no seasickness

Boat entry takes you offshore by boat. It is efficient and reaches good spots, but for anyone prone to seasickness it is a worry from the start. On a choppy day the twenty minutes each way can be miserable, and you feel unwell just when you were supposed to be enjoying the sea. Beach entry means simply walking in from the sand — no boat at all. No motion sickness tablets needed, and no strain on small children, older guests, or anyone who just does not travel well. Every Emerald Reef tour is beach entry.

You can start shallow and build up

The great thing about beach entry is that you can start in zero depth. Practise fitting your mask where you can stand, put your face in, and move deeper only once you are ready. If it feels scary, you can be back on your feet in a second. With boat entry, getting in over deep water, stopping partway or taking a short break is not nearly so simple. For non-swimmers and a child's first time in the sea, beach entry wins easily. As one family put it: "Our daughters love the pool but were frightened of the sea — they never pushed her, and eased her into it so well that by the end each of them was enjoying it their own way."

We can pick a spot by wind direction — even when boats cannot sail

The other big advantage is that we can change the spot to match the day. Wind hits the east and west coasts of Okinawa differently, so we pick a calm place on the sheltered side. Boats are bound by port sailing rules; beach entry can sometimes run on days when boats cannot. One guest wrote: "It was windy and whether we could go was borderline, but they picked a safe spot to match everyone's swimming level." Naturally, if every spot is beyond our safety limits we cancel and offer a new date or a full refund. And to be fair: there are offshore spots only a boat can reach. Choose by what you want from the day.

Even the Blue Cave, from the beach: how the day runs, and prices

For the Blue Cave you enter down the steps at Cape Maeda and swim five to ten minutes to the cave (tell us if you are unsure about the distance). A tour runs three to four hours including travel: hotel pickup, gear fitting, practice in the shallows, then the sea, and afterwards you shower and change in our private tent before we drive you back. Adult prices start at ¥6,000 for snorkeling in Onna, ¥7,000 for the Blue Cave and ¥10,000 for Naha sea turtles (three or more people), with a flat ¥4,000–¥5,000 for children. Mask, fins and life jacket are free, and so is the photo data. Whether boats are not for you or you are not a confident swimmer, just ask.
