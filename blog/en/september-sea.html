Why September Might Be the Best Month to Get in the Water in Okinawa — Peak Sea Temperatures, Thinning Crowds, and Typhoons

Published：2026.09.10
日本語繁體中文한국어

Most people assume the summer holidays are peak season for Okinawa's ocean. But for a guide who is in the water every day, September is the sweet spot: the warmest water of the year, a sea that empties out once the holidays end, and typhoons that are easier to work around than you would think.

The warmest water of the year is September, not August

It surprises people, but Okinawa's sea is at its warmest not in August but from late August into September. Water heats and cools far more slowly than air, so the heat stored through midsummer shows up as the peak water temperature about a month later. In a typical year September water sits around 27–29°C. There is almost no cold shock as you get in, which makes September especially kind to children, older guests and anyone nervous about water. You stay warm even after a long time in the sea, so you can simply take your time down there — the real luxury of this month.

The holidays end and the sea goes quiet

Once mainland schools go back, the sea becomes remarkably quiet from mid-August through mid-September. There are days when you barely cross paths with another group even at popular spots, and far fewer strangers wander into your photos. Emerald Reef tours are always fully private anyway, but when the water itself is empty we get more freedom in choosing where to go, so we can take you to whichever spot is best that day. Bookings are easier to get than in midsummer too — with one exception: Silver Week, when things fill up fast from mid-September.

Gentler sun, less exhausting days

August sun in Okinawa is, frankly, punishing. Come September UV and air temperature ease off and daytime highs settle around 30°C. Warm water with softer sun — that combination is exactly why September feels the most comfortable. Heatstroke risk drops and sunburn is less of a battle, which matters when you are with small children or older family. Underwater, clownfish and schools of damselfish are active and lively, and the feeding experience draws a startling number of fish right up to your mask.

Working around typhoons: how to book in September

Honestly: September is the tail end of typhoon season. But a typhoon arriving does not mean your trip is over. A few days after one passes, the water often clears dramatically as the murk gets flushed away — locals genuinely say the sea is at its most beautiful right after a typhoon. We call off a tour only when conditions exceed our safety limits, and then we offer a new date, or a full refund if that will not work. The trick when booking in September is to put the ocean day in the first half of your stay, leaving room to reschedule.
