Jeju or Okinawa? Why Travelers in Their 20s Pick Okinawa for Their Next Trip (Flights, Costs & the Sea Compared)

Published：2026.07.03
日本語繁體中文한국어

About 2.5 hours by direct flight from Incheon or Busan with no time difference — and a weak yen keeps it affordable. A local guide honestly compares Okinawa with Jeju, from the 30m-visibility sea to Orion beer.

Jeju is great — but sometimes you want something new

You've been to Jeju plenty of times and now you want to use your passport — Okinawa is the perfect next step. It's about as close as Jeju, yet the language, food and streets are all unmistakably Japan. Where volcanic Jeju is about grand nature, Okinawa's star is the emerald sea created by coral reefs — the colour and clarity of the water are clearly different. Add Shuri Castle, Kokusai-dori and American Village, and you get a trip that feels properly "overseas" while staying close to home.

2.5-hour flight, zero time difference, weak yen: what about costs?

Okinawa is about 2.5 hours by direct flight from Incheon or Busan, and with no time difference you can hit the ground running. Catch an LCC sale and airfare can be surprisingly cheap, while the weak yen makes meals and shopping feel like a bargain. Stays range from Naha guesthouses to resort hotels, and short tourist visits are visa-free for Korean passport holders — an overseas trip you can pull off on a long weekend.

The sea really is different: 30m-visibility emerald blue

Okinawa's sea averages about 30m of visibility — clear enough for a boat's shadow to fall on the seabed — with coral reefs, colourful tropical fish, the Blue Cave and wild sea turtles. That's why it's called a mecca for snorkeling and skin diving. The water stays warm, and swimming season runs through summer until around October. One photo in that transparent sea instantly becomes a life-shot. If you're going overseas anyway, come experience a sea that's truly blue.

Food and vibes are no afterthought: Orion beer and Kokusai-dori

What comes after the sea is just as strong. Okinawa soba, taco rice, Blue Seal ice cream — and an Orion draft beer with an ocean view is something else. Graze and souvenir-hunt along Naha's Kokusai-dori, then café-hop and shoot photos in Chatan's American Village, where America and Japan blend on the same street. Finish with a toast at a lantern-lit izakaya. This "Japan × tropics" mood, so different from Jeju, delivers shot after feed-worthy shot.
