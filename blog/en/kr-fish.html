Surrounded by Colorful Fish in Okinawa: What the Feeding Experience Reveals

Published：2026.09.03
日本語繁體中文한국어

"Will I actually see fish while snorkeling?" — a fair worry. On an Emerald Reef tour, the moment feeding starts, dozens of tropical fish swarm right in front of you. Here's why there are so many, and what you'll actually meet, written for anyone in Korea considering Okinawa snorkeling.

"Will I actually see fish?" — that worry

The biggest worry on a first snorkel is whether you'll actually see fish once your face is in the water. The truth is, how many fish you see depends heavily on the spot and whether feeding is part of the tour. Okinawa's coral reefs are some of the clearest in the world, but clear water alone can still leave some guests wanting more. Emerald Reef does two specific things to fix that.

The moment feeding starts, fish surround you

Fish feeding is included in every Emerald Reef plan. When the guide scatters feed in the water, dozens of damselfish and surgeonfish gather within seconds — and suddenly you're surrounded. Watching a swirling school close enough to touch is nothing like seeing it in a photo or video. Guests traveling with kids especially tell us, "I never expected to see fish this close."

The fish you'll meet in Okinawa's ocean

Hundreds of brightly colored tropical fish species live in the waters around Okinawa's main island — the black-and-yellow-striped butterflyfish, the shimmering blue devil damselfish, schools of blue-green chromis, and more. Even first-timers get a scene straight out of a field guide. On some tours, you might get lucky and meet a sea turtle too. The guide names each fish and explains what makes it distinct, so you're not just swimming — you're learning as you go.

A private charter means the fish stay put

On a large shared tour, so many people feed the fish at once that the school can scatter. Every Emerald Reef tour is fully private — with no other group to work around, you feed at your own pace, the fish are less likely to spook, and you get to actually watch them.
