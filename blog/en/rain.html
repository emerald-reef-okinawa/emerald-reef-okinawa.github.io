Can You Snorkel in Okinawa on a Rainy Day? What Really Causes a Cancellation — and Why Rain Can Be a Good Thing

Published：2026.08.14
日本語繁體中文한국어

"The forecast says rain — is the tour cancelled?" In fact, rain is rarely why a snorkel tour gets called off. A local guide explains what actually decides it (swell, wind and lightning), the upsides of a rainy day, and how rescheduling works in typhoon season.

The verdict: yes, you can snorkel in the rain

Straight to the point: tours do run in the rain. People often assume rain means cancellation, but snorkeling gets you completely wet anyway — once you are in the sea you barely notice it falling. Rain does not meaningfully drop the water temperature either; in midsummer a rainy day can actually feel pleasantly cool. The part that surprises people most: underwater is almost unaffected by the weather. Put your face in and the sound of the rain fades — the fish and coral are exactly where they always are. What actually decides a cancellation is not rain, but the swell and wind we explain next.

What really decides it: swell, wind and lightning

Three things decide it: wave height, wind strength and lightning. On days with incoming swell or strong wind, the water clouds up and visibility drops even under blue sky, and the risk of being pushed off position rises — so we cancel or move to a different spot. If there is lightning we stop immediately, even in sunshine: the surface of the water is exactly where you do not want to be, and we never compromise on that. Conversely, a day of light rain with little wind is often beautifully calm underwater — sometimes the best conditions of the week. So "rain in the forecast" does not mean cancelled; we judge by the state of the sea on the day. When a typhoon is approaching we can usually tell days ahead, and we will contact you early.

Three upsides of a rainy day — and one honest caveat

A rainy day actually brings upsides a sunny one does not. ① The sea is quiet — the Blue Cave gets crowded on sunny weekends, but on a rainy day it can be surprisingly empty. ② No need to worry about sunburn — Okinawa's summer UV is genuinely fierce, so an overcast or rainy day is physically easier, which is kinder on kids too. ③ The Blue Cave's blue barely depends on the weather — that glow comes from light entering through the water, so it still shows up beautifully under cloud. One honest caveat, though: after heavy rain, runoff from land can reduce visibility. When that happens we simply guide you to a clearer spot — picking the best water on the day is exactly what a fully private tour lets us do.

Rescheduling, refunds, what to pack — and how to book

If we do have to cancel, we propose a new date first. If your travel plans make that impossible, you get a full refund — no cancellation fee to worry about. We contact you by the day before, and when it is borderline we check in again on the morning. For a rainy day, a spare change of clothes, a big towel, a dry bag and beach sandals make everything easier. Mask, fins and life jacket are free rentals, but we do not provide wetsuits, so bring a rash guard if you feel the cold. Emerald Reef tours are fully private, with free hotel pickup from Naha to Onna Village, free photo data plus video data free for a limited time, and kids from age 3 welcome. The less certain the forecast, the more worth asking — reach us 24/7 via LINE or the contact form, and same-day or next-day bookings are welcome.
